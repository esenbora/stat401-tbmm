
RQ1 – Main Question:
How are written parliamentary questions distributed across ministries, topics, and political parties in the 28th term of TBMM, and how does this distribution evolve over time?
Sub-questions:
Which ministries receive the highest volume of written questions, and how does this ranking shift across legislative years?
What are the dominant latent topics (via LDA / FP-Growth co-occurrence) directed at each ministry, and how do they cluster?
To what extent do parties differ in their topical focus and ministerial targeting, and can a party's identity be predicted from its question portfolio?
Do duplicate or near-duplicate questions (MinHash/LSH similarity ≥ 0.8) exist across MPs or parties, indicating coordinated agenda-setting?
RQ2 – Main Question:
Which provinces of Türkiye receive the most parliamentary attention, on which topics, and can provinces be clustered into latent "attention profiles" based on the issues raised about them?
Sub-questions:
What is the geographic distribution of province mentions across parliamentary documents (transcripts, written questions, research motions)?
Which topics dominate the discourse around each province, and how does this differ between metropolitan vs. rural provinces?
Using K-Means++ clustering on (province × topic) frequency vectors, can we identify distinct provincial profiles (e.g., earthquake-impacted, agricultural, border-security)?
How does provincial attention correlate with population, GDP, or political representation (number of MPs)?
RQ3 – Main Question:
What is the structure of co-sponsorship and co-signing networks among Turkish MPs, and how do PageRank-based influence and Louvain-based community detection align with formal party boundaries?
Sub-questions:
Constructing a co-signing network from legislative proposals and research motions, which MPs emerge as central hubs by PageRank and HITS authority scores?
Do Louvain communities mirror official party affiliations, or do cross-party factions emerge around specific issues?
Using Node2Vec + UMAP embeddings, do MPs cluster more by ideology, geography, or seniority?
Which MPs act as cross-party "bridges" (high betweenness centrality but low intra-party closeness)?
